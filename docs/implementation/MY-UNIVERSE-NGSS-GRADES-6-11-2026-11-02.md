Mani has selected **NGSS for grades 6–11** as the curricular focus. Edmentum course matching is deferred. The application’s demo course/progress entries are not evidence of a learner’s actual enrollment, attainment or current unit.

This refinement makes the existing two missions easier to enter and navigate while preserving their assessed reasoning. It does not publish new missions, change the accepted data or certify full NGSS mastery. The student-device round remains next.

## The learner experience

The repeated pattern is **notice the problem → do one action → record your thinking → Next**. A learner can open a short reference, example or hint when wanted. Going Back keeps work. Practice is separate from the notebook sent to the coach. Shared notebook parts are completed at consolidation points rather than repeatedly demanded after every calculation.

The story should offer curiosity and agency: an energy investigator helping a station, or a planner preparing an instrument delivery. Avoid countdowns, simulated catastrophe, loss of streaks and public comparisons. A learner can request more evidence and still make a strong scientific argument. Difficulty comes from reasoning about the system, not long paragraphs or fast typing.

Example action sequence for wind:

| Card | What the learner sees | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Count the hours | “Add the hours from all five wind bands.” | One practice number, with an optional check |
| Weight each band | “Multiply its hours by its output fraction. Add the products.” | One practice number; optional example |
| Find the energy ratio | “Divide full-power hours by total hours.” | One practice number, then the separate notebook table/words |
| Compare the plans | “Use planning capacity factor 0.34.” | Base comparison, in manageable table groups or words |
| Test less wind | “Change only the capacity factor to 0.20.” | Update the same comparison, keeping earlier work |
| Make a recommendation | “Use your priorities and comparison.” | Choice/reason, then a separate limits/next-measurement response |

These are presentation actions inside the existing authored steps. They are not new assessment requirements. The full original task and coach criterion remain available. Optional help does not reduce outcome eligibility.

The design follows W3C’s supplemental guidance on [clear content](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/objectives/o3-clear-content/): familiar words, short text blocks, clear separation and one instruction at a time. This is an accessibility design rationale, not a claim to treat a condition or proof of WCAG conformance. Twice-exceptional learners vary; learners should choose support and challenge without diagnostic labels.

## Curriculum progression

NGSS groups these expectations into middle school (6–8) and high school (9–12). The recommendations below are our sequence for the requested 6–11 audience, not an official NGSS assignment to individual years. Prior knowledge and learner choice matter more than age. The accepted advanced route retains its historical 9–12 authoring band; the new presentation recommends 9–11 for this pilot.

| Suggested entry | Academic tools | Scientific/engineering demand | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grades 6–7: enter through wind or timing | Read tables, multiply/divide decimals, use ratios and units | Separate data from assumptions; identify limits; compare two or more designs | A traceable calculation and a reason linked to a proposed/agreed criterion |
| Grade 8: deepen the same cases | Weighted sums, percentages, compound unit conversion, inequalities | Compare every option against every constraint; test a changed assumption | A systematic comparison, conditional recommendation and useful next observation |
| Grades 9–10: mass and power | Rearrange equations, exponential calculator use, inverse-square scaling, dimensional checks | Prioritize conflicting criteria and distinguish a partial system model from a complete design | Labeled partial ledger, numerical tradeoff, known omissions and evidence needed |
| Grade 11: defend and transfer | Sensitivity analysis, source comparison, justified precision | Connect a model to real engineering; consider stakeholders and what could reverse a decision | A defensible claim, independent evidence critique and an actionable investigation plan |

Exponential arithmetic is an optional stretch entry, not a prerequisite for all high-school learners or a requirement stated by HS-ETS1-3. Students can develop strong engineering reasoning in the wind/timing routes too. Calculator use is appropriate; speed and memorized formulas are not the learning goal.

## Mission 1: Is the wind worth it?

**Role:** station energy investigator. **Situation:** two teams propose power layouts. **Decision:** recommend Plan A, Plan B or a specific next measurement. **Current challenge:** 2 of 5, provisional. **Recommended entry:** grades 6–8; useful for older learners extending the evidence critique.

Academic content: ratios/percentages, weighted contributions, energy versus power, constraints, units, data quality and controlled comparison. Students distinguish the raw capacity factor from its rounded planning value. Daily energy adequacy must not be described as proof of hourly or nighttime reliability.

The core NGSS connection is [MS-ETS1-2](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ets1-2-engineering-design): evaluating competing designs systematically using jointly developed criteria. The practice is argument from evidence and the disciplinary idea is ETS1.B. This expectation does not specify a separate generic crosscutting concept; we should not invent one merely to fill a badge. Connections between science, technology and society can be discussed without adding an unsupported assessed standard.

Evidence sequence:

1. Identify the synthetic record, assumptions and missing observation.
2. Record constraints and a priority agreed with a coach/group; in solo practice label the priority proposed.
3. Preserve each band’s contribution, total time, full-power-equivalent time and raw/planning ratio in one notebook artifact.
4. Compare **both** plans against energy, mass and deck limits, then test the lower-wind case.
5. Give a conditional choice with a numerical comparison, a limitation and a useful next observation.

The [official evidence statements](https://www.nextgenscience.org/sites/default/files/evidence_statement/black_white/MS-ETS1-2%20Evidence%20Statements%20June%202015%20asterisks.pdf) inform the comparison and missing-evidence prompts. Correct arithmetic alone is insufficient to demonstrate the engineering expectation. The fictional dataset supports rehearsal; actual collaboration and broader transfer need evidence in classroom use.

Optional discussion after the assessed task: “Which missing observation would most change your confidence, and why?” Keep it ungraded unless a new mission version explicitly assesses it. A subsequent real-data mission can test time-resolved supply, demand and storage; it is not part of this pilot’s reliability claim.

## Mission 2: Plan a journey to Ignis

**Role:** expedition planner. **Situation:** compare concepts for an instrument delivery. **Decision:** make a provisional recommendation with a contingency and model limit.

### Timing route — challenge 2, recommended grades 6–8

Academic content: rate relationships, distance/time units, conversion to days, rounding upward for provision days, multipliers and feasibility inequalities. The learner compares chemical, ion and sail cards against arrival and fictional food limits. The qualitative propellant cards cannot justify a calculated propellant ranking.

NGSS connection: supports MS-ETS1-2 through the same evidence-based design comparison. A completed rate worksheet is not enough; the criteria, comparison across all concepts and conditional recommendation carry the engineering reasoning.

A diagram and an ordered text explanation are equal routes to showing the journey. This model supplies average speeds. It does not simulate thrust, acceleration, orbital mechanics or safe flight.

### Mass-and-power route — challenge 4, recommended grades 9–11

Academic content: an ideal exponential mass model, specific impulse as an input, supply allocation, inverse-square flux, efficiency, power per area and a partial mass ledger. A scientific calculator and an optional analogous example support entry. The learner retains responsibility for checking units and explaining the model.

The case uses an **independent 140-day ion duration**. It must remain visibly separate from the timing route’s approximately 69.4-day result. Neither may silently replace the other.

NGSS connection: selected aspects of [HS-ETS1-3](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/hs-ets1-3-engineering-design), including prioritized criteria, constraints, tradeoffs and evaluation of a possible solution. Practice: constructing explanations/designing solutions. DCI: ETS1.B. The standard also connects engineering choices to society and the natural world. The NASA transfer response should identify relevant principles and missing cost, safety, reliability and stakeholder/environmental evidence.

The [high-school evidence statements](https://www.nextgenscience.org/sites/default/files/evidence_statement/black_white/HS-ETS1-3%20Evidence%20Statements%20June%202015%20asterisks.pdf) go beyond formula substitution: a complex real-world problem and independent student-generated evidence matter. This fictional case is a preparation for that work. It does not establish the full expectation, a safe crewed mission, or total launch mass.

## Feedback and review

Use one useful next action in feedback, for example: “Your comparison uses both plans. Next, check whether your chosen plan meets the demand when the wind input changes.” Show the full rubric to the coach, while the learner sees the criterion relevant to the current action.

| Review dimension | Starting | Developing | Supported |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence and units | Values cannot yet be traced | Most inputs/calculations are identifiable; an important unit or distinction is missing | Values and units are traceable, with synthetic/observed/modeled status distinguished |
| Criteria and comparison | Preference without a criterion | Criteria named but some options or constraints missed | All options checked systematically; priorities and agreement/proposal status are clear |
| Reasoning and uncertainty | Choice asserted | Choice uses evidence but omits a consequential limitation | Conditional choice links evidence to priorities and identifies what could change it |
| Revision and communication | Meaning hard to recover | Reasoning partly clear; feedback not yet resolved | Reasoning understandable through an accepted format; relevant feedback addressed |

These are qualitative review anchors, not new server scores, XP, mastery levels or diagnoses. Do not reward verbosity, handwriting neatness, spelling accuracy or speed. Structural character limits are service validation, not measures of reasoning. Keep coach review and science correctness distinct from practice checks.

## Next content extensions — planned, not live

The two pilot missions are predominantly engineering cases. They do not constitute a grades 6–11 science curriculum. Broaden the universe through separately authored, reviewed missions with their own data, versions, criteria and accessible action sequence.

| Future mission | Phenomenon and learner action | Candidate NGSS target and evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Thalassa: What enters the bay? | Investigate how a local process could change water quality; design a monitoring and mitigation plan | [MS-ESS3-3](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ess3-3-earth-and-human-activity). Needs defined human impacts, scientific principles, a monitoring design and evidence that can distinguish cause from correlation. Wind totals alone do not support this claim. |
| Thalassa: Redesign the station footprint | Use independent local evidence to compare and refine a technology that reduces ecological impact | [HS-ESS3-4](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/hs-ess3-4-earth-and-human-activity). Needs measured/credible impact data, student-generated evidence, a refined solution, tradeoffs and stability/change reasoning. This is a new investigation, not an automatic badge for finishing the pilot. |

Before adding any observed dataset, record provenance, date, location at an appropriate privacy level, units, measurement method, uncertainty, reuse permission and limitations. Keep fictional scenario settings distinct from observations about Earth or space. An AI may propose a galaxy or mission; publication requires validated sources, task/version review and technical checks.
