# My Universe — mission atlas

2 November 2026 · Eight worlds · 24 proposed mission scenarios

Companion to the [next-phases plan](MY-UNIVERSE-NEXT-PHASES-2026-11-02.md). **All cards are draft designs, not activated missions or certified NGSS lessons.** Existing draft IDs are preserved where noted. New IDs are proposals and need registry collision checks. Real datasets still need a selected snapshot, attribution, transformations and educator review before use. No synthetic values below are measurements of an actual planet.

## How to use the atlas

The first five worlds belong in the two existing fictional galaxies: Thalassa, Ignis and Boreas remain in Lyra; Verdantia and Cryora would give Aurora its first systems. Solara/Tessera and Lumen are later fictional Forge/Prism proposals. A mission is usable through an accessible evidence workspace even when a custom 3D environment is not ready; the product must explain that availability accurately.

Challenge demand runs from **1 Observe and compare → 2 Investigate → 3 Explain/build → 4 Optimize → 5 Research**. These are editorial task labels, not learner ranks or NGSS grades. Level 1 uses a small supplied set and given criteria; it can include a simple supported choice. Planet ranges below describe only this proposed set. The live product must calculate its displayed range from published missions in the relevant access scope.

Every card offers **Guided** and **Independent** support. Guided supplies a worked method, smaller initial choices and diagram/sentence starters; Independent lets the learner choose more of the method. Extension questions are optional. If a route changes the assessed task or grade band, version and map it separately. Speech, labeled drawings, tables, accessible models and text can be equivalent evidence where the reasoning is preserved. No timed penalties, XP or attendance requirement.

NGSS notes below identify candidate anchors and a proposed three-dimensional focus: **practice (SEP), disciplinary idea (DCI), crosscutting concept (CCC)**. They remain **supports** claims until a named reviewer checks exact elements, evidence statements, boundaries, task and exemplar. Some engineering PEs do not specify a CCC; an added systems/structure lens is instructional integration, not an invented required foundation element. Earth-specific standards require an explicit Earth task; a fictional analogy alone does not establish alignment.

## Thalassa · Lyra Prime · proposed challenge range 1–4

An existing fictional ocean world. Use the platform, weather mast and other existing station locations. Data below comes from clearly identified Earth records or labeled synthetic teaching tables. Waves remain illustrative scenery. Candidate Earth wind/tide evidence can come from [NOAA CO-OPS](https://api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/api/prod/); choose products, station, datum, units and time basis explicitly.

### M01 · Sensor Check

**Proposed ID:** `thalassa-sensor-check` · **Challenge 1** · **Grades 3–5 adaptation** · observation and design comparison.

**Scenario and decision:** The station has two plans for mounting a new wind sensor. One is easy to reach but sheltered by a wall; another has clearer exposure but a less convenient maintenance route. As a junior instrument designer, recommend a mount using the supplied access and measurement criteria.

**Learner actions:** Inspect a labeled station diagram and a small synthetic observation set; separate “easy to service” from “likely to measure the intended wind”; compare both designs against the same criteria; draw a third option or explain a compromise. Guided support models one comparison first.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit an annotated mount drawing and a two-criterion comparison, plus one thing still needing a test. The reviewer looks for a reason connected to evidence rather than the most attractive picture. Revision may ask, “How would we test whether the wall changes the reading?”

**STEAM / NGSS:** Engineering is primary; measurement technology, spatial drawing and categorical comparison have real roles. Supports [3-5-ETS1-2](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/3-5-ets1-2-engineering-design): design solutions; ETS1.B; explicit comparison against criteria. Structure/function is an additional teaching lens. This introductory card does not assess atmospheric circulation.

### M02 · Thalassa Wind Survey

**Existing draft ID to adapt:** `thalassa-wind-survey` · **Challenge 2** · **Grades 6–8** · weighted energy estimates and evidence quality. Preserve `0.1.0-draft` and author a new reviewed version for the additions below; the current draft is titled “Is the wind worth it?” and already teaches full-power hours and capacity factor.

**Scenario and decision:** The weather mast has one fictional week of wind-band records and a simplified turbine output curve. The station wants to know what wind could contribute to its daily energy budget. Estimate that contribution, compare two supplied plans and identify what further evidence is needed before a real installation decision.

**Learner actions:** Add a short evidence-quality prelude checking units, time coverage and measurement notes. Retain the existing core: total the recorded hours; multiply each band's hours by its fractional output; sum full-power hours; divide by total hours for capacity factor. Use that result in the audited Hex-Grid daily-balance scenario, compare the supplied plans against given criteria and identify the limitations of a one-week teaching record. A missing-time or calibration card can invite revision. Real NOAA records would require a separately reviewed preparation/curve method; do not swap them into the fictional table without versioning.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit the weighted calculation table, a labeled graph or equivalent explanation, the daily-balance comparison, a data-quality note and a next-measurement request. A good outcome can be “do not choose an installation yet.” The reviewer checks weighted reasoning, units and the scope of the conclusion. Keep the initial dataset small; an open-ended siting investigation is a separately rated extension.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Mathematical modeling and technology lead; science constrains the conversion, engineering compares plans and visual communication makes limits legible. Supports [MS-ETS1-2](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ets1-2-engineering-design) only with the added systematic design comparison: evaluate solutions; ETS1.B; given criteria. The original arithmetic alone does not assess that PE. Replace placeholder science sources during review. A daily average does not demonstrate night-time supply, hourly reliability or a validated turbine-performance model.

### M03 · Floating City Charter

**Proposed ID:** `thalassa-floating-city-charter` · **Challenge 4** · **Grades 6–8** · interdisciplinary capstone.

**Scenario and decision:** Researchers are designing a small floating settlement that needs energy, freshwater, access and a smaller environmental footprint. There are several viable plans. As the design team, recommend one and explain whose needs and environmental concerns it addresses.

**Learner actions:** Define criteria; compare two layouts and their labeled synthetic daily energy/water budgets; use an audited Hex-Grid activity only for its supported daily energy balance; propose a monitoring plan for a specified discharge or habitat impact; revise after an authored cloudy-day or supply-change case. Reconnect to an Earth coastal settlement example for the environmental claim.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit a site plan, energy/water budget, design comparison, monitoring/mitigation proposal and a short resident-facing charter. Explain one tradeoff, one revision and one model limitation. A beautiful city with an unexplained budget is unfinished evidence.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Engineering and systems science lead; technology supports monitoring, mathematics budgets, and architecture/communication make the settlement understandable and usable. Supports MS-ETS1-2 and [MS-ESS3-3](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ess3-3-earth-and-human-activity): design solutions; ESS3.C; cause/effect. The latter needs an evidence-based Earth monitoring-and-mitigation task. No wave, storm or hourly reliability claim is attached to Hex-Grid.

## Ignis · Lyra Prime · proposed challenge range 2–4

An existing fictional basalt world. Use the sensor station, fissure, columns and gas vent. The scenery does not simulate heat, gas chemistry or eruption forecasts. Curated historical [USGS event data](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/feed/v1.0/geojson.php) can support later Earth pattern tasks, with event revisions retained; it cannot predict a fictional eruption.

### M04 · Plan a Journey to Ignis

**Existing draft ID to preserve:** `spacecraft-journey` · **Challenge 2**, with separately rated advanced variant if needed · **Grades 6–8**.

**Scenario and decision:** An instrument package must travel from Thalassa to an Ignis research site. Two mission concepts differ in travel assumptions, payload, energy and communication opportunities. As mission planners, recommend a feasible concept and make its assumptions visible.

**Learner actions:** Start from the actual existing draft's guided/advanced tasks. For the challenge-2 introductory variant, use given criteria, two supplied plans and one bounded worked calculation; identify an assumption and explain a choice. Learner-defined constraints, multiple interacting calculations and replanning after a payload/communication change belong in a separately rated challenge-3 variant. Keep any new numerical task explicitly fictional until its model is reviewed; do not silently overwrite existing route demands.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit a journey diagram, criteria/constraint table and a reasoned plan with one contingency. A reviewer checks consistency and justification, not whether the learner guessed an unstated preferred route. Do not replace the existing mission's content without inspecting/versioning it.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Engineering is primary, mathematics tests feasibility, technology supplies instruments/communication, and information design explains the plan. Supports [MS-ETS1-1](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ets1-1-engineering-design) and MS-ETS1-2: define/evaluate; ETS1.A/B. A systems lens is instructional. Planning arithmetic is not a validated orbital simulation. Its relationship to Boreas access remains a policy decision, not an automatic outcome.

### M05 · The Missing Sulfur

**Proposed ID:** `ignis-sulfur-model` · **Challenge 3** · **Grades 6–8** · particle models and evidence limits.

**Scenario and decision:** A gas-vent workstation displays different sulfur-containing substances before and after a simplified process. A fictional headline says “the sulfur disappeared.” As scientific illustrators, decide what the supplied model supports and rewrite the headline accurately.

**Learner actions:** Read an educator-authored reaction representation; count sulfur, oxygen and other represented atoms before/after; identify missing participants in an incomplete model; revise it to conserve atoms; distinguish an atom-conservation model from a complete atmospheric mechanism. No mixing or collecting gases is part of this task.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit before/after particle diagrams, a count table and a corrected caption with one unanswered question. Feedback asks whether all modeled atoms are accounted for and whether the learner overclaims what the model explains.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Chemistry leads; mathematical counting and visual representation carry the explanation. Supports [MS-PS1-5](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ps1-5-matter-and-its-interactions): use models; PS1.B; energy/matter through conservation. Do not require symbolic equation balancing or claim a specific SO₂-to-sulfate pathway without specialist review. A pH-only task would not establish MS-PS1-2 alignment.

### M06 · Protect the Instruments

**Proposed ID:** `ignis-thermal-shield` · **Challenge 4** · **Grades 6–8** · tested thermal design.

**Scenario and decision:** A sensor enclosure needs less heat transfer while retaining a clear signal path and a maintenance opening. Materials differ in thermal behavior, mass and cost. Choose and improve a shield rather than maximizing a single property.

**Learner actions:** Predict energy-transfer pathways; compare an educator-provided test set; design a shield; construct and test a safe classroom model or use a reviewed interactive test with recorded controls; combine useful features from competing designs; retest under the same conditions. Teacher-supplied warm/cool data is an equivalent analysis option, with claims adjusted to what was actually tested.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit a cross-section, test method, temperature-time data, revision comparison and limitations. Explain why the improvement is plausible and what a full-scale Ignis design would still need. No learner handles a real high-temperature source.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Engineering and energy science lead; technology measures, mathematics compares trials, and annotated section drawings explain function. Supports [MS-PS3-3](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ps3-3-energy): design/build/test; PS3.A/B; energy/matter. [MS-ETS1-3](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ets1-3-engineering-design) additionally requires using test results to combine useful design characteristics; a materials list alone is insufficient.

## Boreas · Lyra Prime · proposed challenge range 2–4

**Boreas is a fictional ringed ice giant. There is no solid ice landing in this atlas.** Learners work from an orbital/probe workspace or its text/data equivalent. Existing access remains unchanged until the authored requirement and backend policy agree. Earth weather is a comparison case, not a substitute measurement of Boreas.

### M07 · Weather From Orbit

**Proposed ID:** `boreas-weather-evidence` · **Challenge 2** · **Grades 6–8** · weather evidence and uncertainty.

**Scenario and decision:** The orbital team has a cloud-image sequence but cannot tell whether its first explanation is reliable. An Earth training case pairs cloud observations with pressure, temperature, humidity and wind. Recommend a cautious forecast for the Earth case and the next observation needed at Boreas.

**Learner actions:** Collect selected observations from the supplied archive into a time sequence; map the interaction of Earth air masses; compare two explanations; communicate qualitative forecast uncertainty and the evidence behind it; then identify what transfers to the fictional problem and what does not. Use numerical probabilities only when supplied by an identified calibrated model or forecast source, never invented percentages. An optional school cloud log uses an educator-approved observation protocol and prepared-data alternative.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit an annotated sequence, air-mass explanation, forecast uncertainty and a Boreas follow-up request. Feedback checks whether the forecast uses interacting air masses rather than cloud appearance alone. A local observation is not a global weather claim.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Atmospheric science and observation technology lead; mathematics orders measurements and communication design makes the forecast clear. Supports [MS-ESS2-5](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ess2-5-earths-systems): collect/analyze weather evidence; ESS2.C/D; cause/effect. The Earth task carries that mapping. [GLOBE cloud protocols](https://observer.globe.gov/do-globe-observer/clouds) are a candidate reference, not an automatic external upload destination.

### M08 · Choose the Probe

**Proposed ID:** `boreas-probe-design` · **Challenge 3** · **Grades 6–8** · instrument selection.

**Scenario and decision:** The expedition can carry only a limited instrument package. Two explanations for a cloud feature predict different temperature/composition observations. As payload engineers, choose a package that can distinguish the explanations while meeting fictional mass, energy and communication limits.

**Learner actions:** Match instruments to measurable quantities; separate direct measurement from inference; compare at least two payloads; draw the data path back to orbit; revise when a transmission limit changes. All instrument capabilities and resource values must come from a reviewed teaching model.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit an instrument manifest, comparison matrix and a “know / infer / still need” diagram. The key question is which possible result would change the learner's explanation. An instrument that produces impressive pictures but no discriminating evidence need not be the best choice.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Engineering and technology lead; science defines the question, mathematics budgets resources and visual communication explains the instrument/data relationship. Supports MS-ETS1-1/2: defining/evaluating; ETS1.A/B. The systems/measurement lens is explicit teaching content. This mission does not certify atmospheric chemistry expertise or unlock Boreas from a local click.

### M09 · Redesign the Wind Map

**Proposed ID:** `boreas-circulation-model` · **Challenge 4** · **Grades 6–8** · model revision.

**Scenario and decision:** A training model predicts the same wind at every latitude. The team suspects missing causes. Build a better Earth circulation explanation, then identify what would need checking before using an analogous model for Boreas.

**Learner actions:** Compare Earth heating and circulation evidence; model uneven solar heating, rotation, atmospheric/ocean circulation and relevant geographic effects; identify a regional prediction; compare against a supplied case; revise and state limits of the analogy. Guided support builds one causal relationship at a time.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit a causal diagram, before/after model comparison and a prediction with supporting evidence. Reviewers look for connected mechanisms rather than a memorized arrow map. Learners do not need to calculate Coriolis dynamics.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Systems science leads, with mapping technology, proportional reasoning and diagram design. Supports [MS-ESS2-6](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ess2-6-earths-systems): develop/use a model; ESS2.C/D; systems/system models. Unequal heating and rotation must both be represented. Boreas scenery cannot stand in for Earth evidence.

## Verdantia · proposed Aurora system · proposed challenge range 1–4

A fictional ecological field station with different connected habitats. Organisms, population values and story changes are authored fiction. Earth ecology cases must retain their real sampling methods and limits. A later thermal-landscape mission can use a prepared [Landsat surface-temperature subset](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-collection-2-surface-temperature), explicitly distinguished from air temperature or personal heat exposure.

### M10 · The Empty Pollinator Path

**Proposed ID:** `verdantia-pollinator-path` · **Challenge 1** · **Grades 3–5 adaptation** · noticing evidence and asking a better question.

**Scenario and decision:** A field guide shows few pollinator sightings on one path. Is the habitat unsuitable, or was it observed differently? As habitat observers, decide what the records justify and design one useful follow-up observation.

**Learner actions:** Compare a small set of habitat cards, organism needs and observation notes; spot different observation effort/time; match a documented Earth organism's needs to two Earth habitat cases; offer a supported habitat argument and a missing-evidence question. Do not infer population decline from a short list of sightings.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit an observation map and a claim with evidence, an alternative explanation and a follow-up. A drawing or oral explanation is sufficient if the reasoning is clear. Feedback distinguishes “we did not see it” from “it cannot survive here.”

**STEAM / NGSS:** Life science leads; sampling, simple counts and accessible field-guide illustration support it. Supports [3-LS4-3](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/3-ls4-3-biological-evolution-unity-and-diversity): argument from evidence; LS4.C; cause/effect. The Earth habitat evidence must connect needs to survival; counts alone only support preparatory observation practices.

### M11 · The Population Mystery

**Proposed ID:** `verdantia-population-mystery` · **Challenge 3** · **Grades 6–8** · ecosystem evidence arguments.

**Scenario and decision:** A population changes while rainfall, habitat and another species also change. The station has three plausible explanations. As ecosystem investigators, argue for the explanation best supported by a curated Earth case, including what remains uncertain.

**Learner actions:** Check sampling effort and units; graph population/environment records; model relevant ecosystem relationships; compare explanations and a counterexample; request an observation that could weaken the preferred explanation. Apply the reasoning to the fictional field station without claiming its data is real.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit an evidence-based argument, graph/model, counterargument and uncertainty statement. Feedback rewards identifying a genuine evidence gap. Correlation is insufficient to declare a cause; inconsistent data can justify a conditional conclusion.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Ecology and data reasoning lead; technology organizes observations and communication makes the argument inspectable. Supports [MS-LS2-4](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ls2-4-ecosystems-interactions-energy-and-dynamics): argument from empirical evidence; LS2.C; stability/change. The required ecosystem mechanism and empirical evidence must be in the reviewed task.

### M12 · Build a Living Corridor

**Proposed ID:** `verdantia-living-corridor` · **Challenge 4** · **Grades 6–8** · ecological design.

**Scenario and decision:** A proposed settlement divides two habitats. Several corridor plans differ in width, access, resources and likely ecological usefulness. As landscape designers, recommend a plan and explain how it will be tested after construction.

**Learner actions:** Define organism and human needs; compare sourced Earth design evidence; evaluate at least two plans; propose indicators and a comparison site; revise when a maintenance/access constraint changes. Use the Earth case to justify monitoring and mitigation rather than assuming a green-looking map is beneficial.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit a corridor map, design comparison, causal explanation, monitoring plan and resident-facing guide. Include a limitation such as species differences or seasonal sampling. Explain how an apparently unsuccessful result would change the design.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Ecology and engineering combine; mapping, quantitative tradeoffs and inclusive landscape/graphic design matter. Supports MS-ETS1-2 and MS-ESS3-3: evaluate/design; ETS1.B/ESS3.C; cause/effect for the environmental task. A decorative planting plan without evidence or monitoring is not sufficient.

## Cryora · proposed Aurora system · proposed challenge range 2–4

A **new fictional rocky world with ice**, not a redefinition of Boreas. Its station supports images, thermal investigations and environmental planning. A custom ice environment needs a reviewed capability release; until then, use accessible diagrams and data.

### M13 · Two Ice Maps

**Proposed ID:** `cryora-two-ice-maps` · **Challenge 2** · **Grade 5 adaptation** · comparing evidence and systems models.

**Scenario and decision:** Two maps appear to disagree about the ice edge near a station. As archive investigators, decide whether the disagreement shows a real change or comes from date, season, scale or measurement differences.

**Learner actions:** Inspect prepared Earth images with dates, scale and uncertainty; match comparable observations; distinguish land ice from sea ice; model an interaction between the hydrosphere and one other Earth system; state which conclusions require a longer record. Avoid making a climate trend from two unmatched images.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit an annotated image pair and a two-system explanation, with a request for another observation. Reviewers look for comparable evidence and a mechanistic relationship, not a dramatic before/after caption.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Earth systems and measurement lead; spatial mathematics and visual annotation make comparisons fair. Supports [5-ESS2-1](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/5-ess2-1-earths-systems): develop models; ESS2.A; systems/system models. Keep the assessment to two Earth systems at a time. Image comparison by itself does not assess a climate-change PE.

### M14 · Keep the Sample Cold

**Proposed ID:** `cryora-cold-chain` · **Challenge 3** · **Grades 6–8** · thermal design and fair testing.

**Scenario and decision:** A sample needs a transport container that reduces warming, can be opened with limited dexterity and uses bounded materials. Choose and improve an insulation design under the same test conditions.

**Learner actions:** Predict thermal energy transfer; define criteria; construct/test a safe model with an educator-approved method or reviewed digital equivalent; hold relevant conditions constant; compare trials; revise the container and explain the transfer pathway. Do not use dry ice, cryogens or inaccessible field equipment.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit a section drawing, fair-test method, data and before/after explanation. The reviewer checks whether the conclusion matches the tested conditions. With supplied data only, describe the outcome as design analysis rather than claiming the learner constructed/tested a real device.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Energy science and engineering lead; sensing, graphs and accessible product design are integral. Supports MS-PS3-3: design/build/test; PS3.A/B; energy/matter. A separately authored grades 3–5 route can support [3-5-ETS1-3](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/3-5-ets1-3-engineering-design) through controlled fair tests and identified improvements; it needs its own task mapping.

### M15 · Research Base Charter

**Proposed ID:** `cryora-base-charter` · **Challenge 4** · **Grades 6–8** · environmental systems design.

**Scenario and decision:** A small research base needs warmth, freshwater and low disturbance to its surroundings. Choose a layout and supply strategy while identifying which effects should be monitored and minimized.

**Learner actions:** Compare two base plans and synthetic resource budgets; develop an explicit Earth water-cycle model including energy from sunlight and gravity; identify a relevant human impact in an Earth cold-region case; design monitoring/mitigation; revise after an authored supply interruption. Keep the fictional base and Earth evidence clearly separated.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit a base plan, water/energy flow diagram, comparison, monitoring proposal and one revision. A strong explanation shows where water moves/stores and why an intervention could reduce the named impact.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Engineering and Earth systems combine with sensing, budgets and architecture. Supports [MS-ESS2-4](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ess2-4-earths-systems) through the Earth water-cycle model, plus MS-ESS3-3 through the Earth impact task. The DCI/CCC focus is ESS2.C and energy/matter for the water model; ESS3.C and cause/effect for mitigation. These are distinct evidence strands, not one generic systems badge.

## Solara · proposed Forge system · proposed challenge range 1–4

A fictional solar-resource world connecting observation to practical design. Audit existing solar-car, sun-power and energy activities before embedding them. Candidate Earth resource series come from [NASA POWER daily data](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/daily/), with grid resolution, dates, units and time basis retained. A resource dataset is not an electrical-grid simulator.

### M16 · A Place for Shade

**Proposed ID:** `solara-shade-design` · **Challenge 1** · **Grades 3–5 adaptation** · comparing designs for people.

**Scenario and decision:** A station walkway needs shade while preserving visibility and access. Two canopy sketches solve different parts of the problem. As public-space designers, compare them and suggest an improvement.

**Learner actions:** Read a simple sun/shadow diagram and safe prepared observations; compare coverage, access and material constraints; draw an improved option; propose one fair test. Optional classroom observations use approved equipment and never involve looking directly at the Sun.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit a plan with a criterion-by-criterion comparison and a testable prediction. Feedback asks whose route is shaded, whether access is maintained and what the diagram cannot tell us about air temperature or heat exposure.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Design is primary; light/shadow observation, simple area comparison and spatial communication support it. Supports 3-5-ETS1-2: design solutions; ETS1.B; comparison of alternatives. Structure/function is a teaching lens. A shade drawing alone does not establish a thermal-energy standard.

### M17 · Follow the Energy

**Proposed ID:** `solara-energy-budget` · **Challenge 3** · **Grades 6–8** · resource planning.

**Scenario and decision:** A settlement needs to run a water pump and communication beacon. Different plans use different panel areas and optional loads. As energy planners, choose a plan and explain where its assumptions could fail.

**Learner actions:** Read a curated Earth solar-resource series; identify gaps and units; apply an educator-reviewed conversion with explicit efficiency assumptions; distinguish power from daily energy; compare two daily supply/demand budgets; revise one plan and communicate a load-priority choice. Do not model storage by silently treating a daily total as an hourly series.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit an energy-flow diagram, calculations, criteria table and uncertainty note. Feedback checks unit consistency and whether the explanation identifies unmodeled night-time demand or variability.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Mathematics and engineering lead, grounded in energy science and data technology; diagram design explains the system. Supports MS-ETS1-2: evaluate alternatives; ETS1.B; an energy/matter lens enriches instruction. Calculating a budget alone does not assess an energy-conservation PE. An audited Hex-Grid adapter may be reusable within its steady daily-balance limits.

### M18 · Power Through Change

**Proposed ID:** `solara-resilient-power` · **Challenge 4** · **Grades 6–8** · design iteration and tradeoffs.

**Scenario and decision:** A research settlement wants reliable essential services with fair access during variable solar conditions. No option wins on every criterion. Recommend a plan that makes its tradeoffs and service limits explicit.

**Learner actions:** Compare tested plan outputs; combine useful features; stress-test a revised plan against an unseen authored resource case; consider essential versus optional loads and maintenance constraints; explain who is affected by each choice. Use a separately reviewed time-step/storage model if hourly reliability is actually assessed; otherwise keep claims to daily energy adequacy.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit baseline/revised plans, test results, a tradeoff matrix and an accessible settlement notice. Feedback asks whether the revised plan actually improves the stated criteria and whether uncertainty is communicated honestly.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Systems engineering leads; mathematics evaluates, technology controls, science constrains and communication design makes distribution choices visible. Supports MS-ETS1-3 through analysis and combination of tested designs; ETS1.B/C. A systems lens is additional instruction. Do not declare a plan reliable across weather or hours its model does not represent.

## Lumen · proposed Prism system · proposed challenge range 2–5

A fictional observatory world linked to a clearly separate view of real astronomy. Use selected, referenced [NASA Exoplanet Archive records](https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/TAP/usingTAP.html) and curated observation products. Archive composite values can come from different studies; retain their references and do not assume every row is a self-consistent planetary model. Candidate, confirmed and synthetic training signals have separate labels.

### M19 · The Dimming Star

**Proposed ID:** `lumen-dimming-star` · **Challenge 2** · **Grades 6–8** · pattern evidence and alternative explanations.

**Scenario and decision:** A star's measured brightness dips. Is it a transit candidate, stellar variation, an instrument effect or insufficient evidence? As observation analysts, recommend a classification for review and a useful next observation.

**Learner actions:** Inspect repeated light-curve segments and a control; check gaps and relative-brightness axes; compare possible explanations; mark what distinguishes them; propose a follow-up measurement. Begin with synthetic examples labeled as such, then a small reviewed archived case. A learner does not confirm or name a real planet.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit an annotated light curve, alternative-explanation table and follow-up request. “Insufficient evidence” is acceptable when justified. Feedback focuses on the discriminating evidence and data quality.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Data science and scientific observation lead; mathematics identifies repetition and communication design distinguishes data from interpretation. The primary goal is an NGSS practice progression—analyzing evidence and comparing explanations—with an instructional patterns lens; **no full astronomy PE is claimed**. A separately added instrument-design task could support MS-ETS1-1/2, but pattern matching alone does not. Borrow pedagogy, not community features, from [NASA Planet Hunters TESS](https://science.nasa.gov/citizen-science/planet-hunters-tess/).

### M20 · Send a Clear Signal

**Proposed ID:** `lumen-clear-signal` · **Challenge 3** · **Grades 6–8** · communication technology.

**Scenario and decision:** The observatory must send a short image/message through a noisy channel. Two methods encode the information differently. As communication designers, recommend a method using qualitative technical evidence and a controlled teaching demonstration.

**Learner actions:** Compare an analog representation and digitized wave-pulse representation; observe the same authored disturbance applied to both; examine a short technical source; explain why recoverable discrete states can improve reliability under the stated conditions; design a readable diagram or sound/text demonstration. Do not claim digital communication is immune to noise.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit a channel diagram, demonstration record and an argument combining technical text with the visual result. Feedback asks whether the explanation connects structure to function and states the conditions where the demonstration holds.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Technology and waves lead; accessible visual/audio representation is part of the communication design. Supports [MS-PS4-3](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ps4-3-waves-and-their-applications-technologies-information-transfer): obtain/evaluate/communicate; PS4.C; structure/function. Binary counting and detailed device mechanisms are outside its assessment boundary; a coding quiz is not the target.

**Scenario and decision:** A museum at the observatory needs an explanation of why scientists support a hot, expanding early-universe model. As research curators, build an exhibit that connects several independent observations and explains what each does—and does not—show.

### M22 · Material Detective

**Proposed ID:** `tessera-material-detective` · **Challenge 2** · **Grade 5 adaptation** · material identification.

**Scenario and decision:** Labels have come off several workshop samples. The station needs a suitable cover for a small instrument, but color alone is misleading. As material investigators, identify the candidates using a combination of observable properties, then justify a choice.

**Learner actions:** Plan safe observations or use a teacher-supplied test station; compare properties such as hardness, reflectivity, response to a magnet and permitted conductivity results; record measurements with appropriate units; narrow the identities; state which observation would resolve a remaining ambiguity. Do not taste, burn or smell unknown samples.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit a property table, identification reasoning and a labeled design recommendation. Feedback asks whether multiple properties support the identification and whether the proposed use follows from measured properties.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Materials science leads; measurement tools, structured comparison and clear sample labeling have real roles. Supports [5-PS1-3](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/5-ps1-3-matter-and-its-interactions): investigate/measure; PS1.A; scale/proportion/quantity. Do not use density or require distinguishing mass and weight in the grade-5 assessment.

### M23 · A Material's Life

**Proposed ID:** `tessera-material-life` · **Challenge 3** · **Grades 6–8** · scientific information and societal impact.

**Scenario and decision:** A workshop advertises a new synthetic material as “impact free.” The settlement wants a more honest material label. As evidence editors, trace its natural-resource origin, the chemical transformation that makes it and the effects of its use and disposal.

**Learner actions:** Evaluate a curated packet from suitable primary/technical sources; trace the resource-to-material process qualitatively; distinguish chemical manufacture from simple cutting/shaping; explain which resulting material properties suit the intended product function; compare product claims with evidence; explain one benefit, impact and unresolved issue. No industrial chemistry is conducted by the learner.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit a material journey diagram, source audit and revised product label for a real audience. Feedback checks that the sources support the actual material/process and that environmental claims state their scope.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Chemistry and information literacy lead; lifecycle representation and communication design are essential. Supports [MS-PS1-3](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ps1-3-matter-and-its-interactions): obtain/evaluate/communicate; PS1.A/B; structure/function. The task must include natural resources chemically transformed into synthetic materials and societal effects; a recycling poster alone is insufficient.

### M24 · City Without a Bin

**Proposed ID:** `tessera-circular-city` · **Challenge 4** · **Grades 6–8** · circular-system design.

**Scenario and decision:** Tessera wants to reduce disposal while keeping essential supplies usable and accessible. Its first plan assumes every item can be recycled forever. As systems designers, replace that claim with a feasible repair, reuse and recovery plan.

**Learner actions:** Audit a synthetic material-flow table; distinguish reduce/reuse/repair/recycle and unavoidable losses; compare two collection/workshop plans; link a real Earth waste-system impact to a proposed intervention and monitoring indicator; revise after an authored contamination or transport constraint. Explain where additional process evidence is needed.

**Evidence and feedback:** Submit a material-flow diagram, design comparison, monitoring/mitigation proposal and a readable resident guide. Feedback checks mass-flow consistency, explicit losses, local-system assumptions and a response to contrary evidence. “Zero waste” is a hypothesis to test, not the expected answer.

**STEAM / NGSS:** Engineering and environmental science combine with material data, quantitative flow reasoning and service/graphic design. Supports MS-ETS1-2 and MS-ESS3-3: evaluate/design; ETS1.B/ESS3.C; cause/effect for the Earth impact task. Local recycling feasibility must be researched rather than invented by AI.

## One complete learner session: the wind survey

1. **Arrival:** open Thalassa and choose the weather mast. The scene and briefing say the world is fictional; the evidence drawer labels the selected Earth dataset or synthetic teaching set.
2. **Briefing:** “What could a week of wind contribute to the station's daily energy budget, and what can it not tell us?” The learner sees the artifact expected before starting.
3. **Support choice:** Guided demonstrates one hours-times-output calculation; Independent begins with the supplied wind-band table. Both can switch support without penalty.
4. **Investigation:** check provenance/time coverage, calculate full-power hours and capacity factor, and explain why averaging speed first is not equivalent to using the supplied nonlinear output curve. Apply the result only within the audited daily-balance model.
5. **Decision:** compare two supplied daily-energy plans against given criteria, or request better evidence before recommending an installation. State that daily adequacy does not prove night-time reliability.
6. **Communication:** submit the artifact, evidence reference and limitation through the versioned mission workflow. Local completion does not approve the work.
7. **Feedback:** the coach asks for a missing units label or accepts a sound argument; the learner can revise. AI may explain a prompt or suggest what to check, without fabricating a reading or approving the result.
8. **Next possibility:** the service returns the approved mission outcome. It may make a policy-approved expedition available or simply mark the investigation reviewed. The next suggestion explains why the floating-city task uses this evidence. No XP or public ranking appears.

## Authoring and activation checklist

Before any card is published, its owner must supply the actual dataset/model, stable task version, complete learner instructions, expected artifacts, rubric/exemplar, alternative accessible format, exact standards mapping, content/science reviewer decision and intended access outcome. The service must verify compatible renderer/workstation capabilities and release references.

Do not publish a planet with a list of mission titles but no usable tasks. Do not claim that this atlas alone implements a curriculum. For the pilot, complete **M02 and M04** first using the existing drafts; then test one introductory route and one more demanding design route before expanding the catalog.

All 24 cards are starting briefs for co-design with educators and learners. Their value is the decision and reasoning they invite, not the number of destinations they add.
