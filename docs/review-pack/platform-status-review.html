At a glance

Where the platform is today

WeLearn Growth Hub is being built as one learning platform for all our cohorts: students open their courses, the PBL/STEAM simulations and other materials in one place, and staff, teachers and parents each see what they need. Phase 0 of the implementation plan (the screens and groundwork that need nothing from the school) is now finished and tested.

Important: nothing in this pack is live yet. Every screenshot was taken from the platform running on a test system with made-up accounts (for example "Mia Chen", "Bangkok Campus (test)"). The live website has not been changed. Going live needs the school actions on page 22, starting with the Firebase billing plan.

How to give feedback

Every screen has a number (for example 6.4). Please refer to it in your comments, and use the feedback form on the last page. Each screen also carries one question we would especially like your view on. Comments on wording, what is missing, what confuses you, and what you would use every day are all welcome.

Signing in

1
1.1

Sign in

Working

One sign-in page for every role. The platform decides what each person sees from their account, so nobody has to choose "student" or "teacher". Language can be switched before signing in. Wrong passwords get a plain message, not a technical code.

Question: Should students be able to create their own accounts here, or should every account be created by the school?

1.2

Forgot password

Working

Sends a real password-reset email. It gives the same answer whether or not the email has an account, so it cannot be used to find out who is enrolled.

My Courses (the student's home page)

Partly done

Where students land after signing in: every course and activity in one place, with search. The eight WeLearn PBL/STEAM activities open today. Primary learners, ages 6–11

Junior Explorers

3
3.1

Junior Explorers home: all eight activities

Working

All eight primary activities are playable: Hot Spots, Storm Shelter, Micro:bit Blocks, Mangroves vs Waves, Sun Power Race, Acid Rain Detectives, Plant Microscope Lab and Floating House. Each one is a real hands-on investigation with the school's kit. Read-aloud and big text are on by default. Two levels: Starter (6–8) and Explorer (9–11).

Question: Do the activities match the kit and space you have? Is anything unsafe or unrealistic for your class?

3.2

An activity: build first, then tests

Working

Every activity starts with a build or setup, then two or three fair tests. Children can print their worksheet from here.

3.3

Get ready

Tick off the kit. No typing anywhere: children tap.

3.4

My guess

Choose a prediction and how sure they are.

3.5

Look and record: a real temperature

Working

Readings from the micro:bit thermometer are entered with big + and − buttons. There is also a stopwatch for timed tests (Explorer level).

3.6

What happened

Working

The result is written only from what the child recorded ("In the sun it was 38 °C. In the shade it was 31 °C."). The platform suggests whether the guess was right, and the child decides.

Question: Is the language right for 6–8-year-olds? What would you change?

Hands-on worksheets and teacher guides

4
4.1

Primary resources

Working

For every primary activity: a pupil worksheet (Starter and Explorer versions) and a teacher guide. They are generated from the same content as the app, so paper and screen always match. Ready-made PDFs of all 24 are available.

4.2

Pupil worksheet

Circle a guess, tick steps, record results, draw, plus an Art corner and a Maths corner. Starter has no writing lines.

4.3

Teacher guide

Goals, what each STEAM letter means here, preparation, safety, a timed lesson plan, discussion questions and what to look for.

Question: Lesson timings and group sizes are estimates. Please correct them to our timetable, and confirm the curriculum links marked "proposed".

Administrators and supervisors

Administration

6
6.1

Students

Working

Search by name, email or ID, filter by cohort and status, sort any column. Each person gets a unique ID. Pending (not yet activated) and suspended accounts are clearly marked.

6.2

Staff

Administrators, supervisors and course coordinators.

6.3

Parents

With the number of children each parent is linked to.

6.4

One account

Working

Details, linked parents, and actions: edit details, change email, issue a parent invite code, suspend. Every change is recorded in the account history, which nobody can edit or delete.

6.5

Account history

Working
6.6

Parent invite code

Parents link to a child with a one-time code from the school, never by typing an email, which would let anyone attach themselves to any child. Codes expire after 7 days.

6.7

Suspend an account

A reason is required and kept in the history. The person is signed out everywhere at once.

6.8

Add a student

Partly done

Staff never choose or see a password: the new user gets an email to set their own. Until the school's Microsoft 365 no-reply mailbox exists, no email is sent, and the screen says so honestly. Primary learners stay "pending" until a parent gives consent.

Question: Which details must we collect for each student, and which should we not collect at all?

6.9

Supervisor view (read-only)

Working

Supervisors see the accounts in their own schools and the history, but cannot change anything.

Question: What else do supervisors need: reports, class overviews, usage?

Not built yet in administration: managing schools and classes, the course catalogue and course editor, the gradebook and transcripts, and Edmentum settings. These are Phases A to D.
